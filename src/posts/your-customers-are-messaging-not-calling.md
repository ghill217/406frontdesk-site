Ask a tattoo artist, a groomer or a stylist where their new business comes in, and almost none of them will say "the phone." It comes in as an Instagram DM. A Facebook message. A text to a number that used to be a landline.

Plenty of these businesses don't even have a website. They have an Instagram page, a phone, and a full book.

If that's you, most advice about "never missing a call" is solving a problem you don't have — while the actual problem sits unread in three different inboxes.

## Why messages are worse than calls, operationally

A missed call at least announces itself. There's a voicemail, or a number in your recents. You know something happened.

Messages are quieter and, in a few specific ways, harder:

**They arrive with nothing attached.** "How much for a sleeve?" No size, no placement, no reference. Answering it properly means asking the same four questions you've asked a thousand times, then waiting.

**They're spread across apps.** Instagram, Messenger, texts, maybe the website. Each one has its own unread badge, and it's genuinely easy to lose a real enquiry between them for two days.

**They feel less urgent than they are.** A ringing phone interrupts. A DM sits there looking patient — and the person who sent it is messaging three other shops while they wait.

**The reply window is short.** Somebody messaging four studios at 9 PM is going with whoever answers first with something useful. Not the best portfolio. The first real reply.

## The hands-busy problem

The reason none of this gets handled isn't laziness. It's that the people who could answer are physically unable to.

A tattoo artist is gloved up for four hours. A groomer has both hands on a wet dog. A stylist is mid-colour. A massage therapist is ninety minutes into a session with the phone deliberately silent.

These aren't jobs where you glance at your phone between tasks. The work *is* the block. And the busier you are — which is to say, the better things are going — the deeper the pile gets.

Then the day ends, and answering forty messages is the thing standing between you and going home.

## What a front desk has to do now

If the enquiries arrive as messages, then the front desk has to live where the messages are. In practice that means:

**It answers in the channel they used.** Instagram DMs, Facebook messages, texts, website chat. Not "leave your number and we'll call you back" — that's asking someone who chose to type to switch to a medium they were avoiding.

**It asks the qualifying questions for you.** By the time you look, the size, placement, breed, coat condition or service is already captured. You're reading a brief, not starting a conversation.

**It answers the routine stuff outright.** Deposits, shop minimum, whether you're taking new clients, parking, hours, whether you work on that breed or that make. These don't need you. They just need to be answered before the person moves on.

**It follows up with the ones who went quiet.** Somebody asked a question at 10 PM, got an answer, and never replied. A single friendly nudge the next day recovers more of those than most owners expect, and nobody has to remember to send it.

**It hands you the ones that need you.** A cover-up question, a complaint, an unusual request — those come to you with the conversation attached.

## What it must not do

The same limits apply here as anywhere, and they matter more in a DM because the conversation feels casual:

- **It doesn't quote custom work.** It captures the variables and tells them you'll confirm.
- **It doesn't give an opinion on the work.** Whether a design suits a spot, whether a colour will lift, whether a dog's skin condition is a problem — that's you.
- **It sounds like your business.** Deposit terms, what you will and won't take on, your policies in your words. A generic chatbot voice in a DM is instantly obvious and worse than a slow reply.

## Where the website fits

If you don't have one, this still works. The receptionist can run entirely on your DMs and your phone.

A site helps later — it's a surface you own, it gives you somewhere to send people, and it's the difference between showing up on Google and existing only inside someone else's app. But it isn't a prerequisite, and anyone who tells you that you need to build a website before you can stop losing enquiries has the order backwards.

Fix the leak first. Build the house when you're ready.
